# Delivery Horizon Events & Techniques

The delivery horizon is where the four familiar agile events happen. Their names are easy
to remember and their purposes easy to confuse, and that is exactly what an assessment
exploits: it describes an activity that **genuinely does happen** on an agile team and asks
which event it belongs to. The wrong answers are always **another real event**.

The way through is to learn each event as two halves: **what it is for** and **what it is
NOT for**.

## The four events, separated

| Event | What it is for | What it is NOT for |
|---|---|---|
| **Backlog refinement** | Analysing, prioritising and preparing backlog items; reviewing priorities and removing items; story elaboration and story decomposition | Identifying the **detailed tasks** needed to complete each item — that belongs to the planning workshop |
| **Planning workshop** | Planning the **tasks** needed to complete the upcoming iteration (at delivery); feature order and the release plan (at initiative); goals and metrics (at strategy) | Discussing **detailed design options** — the team should have discussed those already, during story elaboration |
| **Review** | Showing an **increment of the completed, working solution** to users and stakeholders to get feedback on its alignment to the user need | Getting **approval**; reviewing code; reviewing the plan for what is next |
| **Retrospective** | **Explicitly discussing** opportunities for continuous improvement of the process and the way of working | Producing a list of completed stories, an updated Kanban board, or a task list per person |

A reading heuristic: if the question describes **looking forward** (preparing, planning) it
is refinement or planning; if it describes **looking back** it is the review (looking back
at the product) or the retrospective (looking back at the way of working).

## Backlog refinement versus the planning workshop

This is the most frequently swapped pair of the four, and the boundary is sharp:

- **Backlog refinement** answers: *what is this item, is it worth doing, how big is it,
  where does it sit in the queue?* This is where stories are elaborated and decomposed.
- **The planning workshop** answers: *to complete the items we have chosen, what tasks does
  the team need to do in this iteration?*

So the question *"what does the BA need to help the team AVOID during backlog refinement?"*
has the answer **identifying the detailed tasks needed to complete each backlog item** —
tasks are identified in a planning workshop.

The reverse also holds: **backlog refinement is the key input to a planning workshop**.
Without a refined backlog, the planning session has nothing to plan.

Three things that genuinely **do** belong to backlog refinement, and that show up as
distractors: analysing/prioritising/preparing items; reviewing priorities and removing
items; story elaboration and story decomposition.

## The review: what is demonstrated, and what for

In a review, what is demonstrated is **an increment of the completed solution** — working
product. Three things it is not:

- **UI mockups** — they do not demonstrate anything working.
- **The code** — a piece behind the scenes, not working product.
- **Configuration tables** — also behind the scenes, not working product.

And **why** teams hold reviews: to **get feedback from stakeholders on the increment's
alignment to user needs**. Three purposes it is not:

- **Getting approval** — a review is not an approval gate; feedback is given, but for
  learning and dialogue, not sign-off.
- **Peer-reviewing code** — not part of a review as defined by the Agile Extension v2.
- **Reviewing the plan for what is next** — also not part of a review.

These two questions — *what is shown* and *what it is for* — appear separately, so learn
both halves.

## The retrospective: what its output is

The retrospective is the delivery-horizon technique used as a means of **explicitly
discussing opportunities for continuous improvement**. That phrase is the identifying cue;
if it appears in the stem, you need not read the other three options.

**The key output of a retrospective is a list of action items.** Continuous improvement
means action. Three things it is not:

- **A list of completed stories** — this may be an **input** to a retrospective, but never
  its output.
- **An updated Kanban board** — not a retrospective output.
- **A task list per person** — some tasks may be determined, but that is not the main output
  and not what makes a retrospective good.

The retrospective is also where the **Stimulate collaboration and continuous improvement**
principle shows most clearly. If a question pairs an event with a principle, the correct
pair is *Retrospective + Stimulate collaboration and continuous improvement*.

## Story elaboration and design conversations

**Detailed design options are discussed during story elaboration, BEFORE the iteration
begins** — not in the planning workshop, not in the review, not in the retrospective. By the
time the team walks into a planning session those design choices should already have been
made; planning only splits the work into tasks.

Story elaboration lives mostly at the **delivery horizon**, although backlog refinement at
the initiative horizon does include some elaboration of features and creation of stories.

## Observation

**Observation** is the delivery-horizon technique used to **understand how users actually
perform a task or use the solution**. Three techniques are commonly offered against it:

| Technique | What it does — and why it is not the answer |
|---|---|
| **User stories** | Help communicate and analyse user tasks, but do not help you *understand* those tasks as well as watching them |
| **Acceptance criteria** | Define the boundaries of a story and help verify and validate that the solution met the need — not an investigative tool |
| **Purpose alignment model** | Assesses ideas in the context of customer and business value — a strategy-horizon technique |

In short: when the question's verb is **"understand how users work"**, the answer is
observation. When the verb is **"communicate"** or **"validate"**, the answer is a story or
acceptance criteria.

## Key takeaways

- The four events have sharply separated purposes; the wrong answer is always another real
  event.
- **Detailed tasks → planning workshop**, not backlog refinement. **Detailed design → story
  elaboration**, before the iteration.
- Backlog refinement is the **input** to a planning workshop, and it is **continuous**.
- A review demonstrates a **completed, working increment** to **get feedback** — not
  mockups, not code, and not for approval.
- The output of a retrospective is a **list of action items**. A list of completed stories
  is an input, not an output.
- **Observation** is the technique for understanding how users actually work.
