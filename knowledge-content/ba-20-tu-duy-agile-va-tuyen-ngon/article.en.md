# The Agile Mindset & the Four Manifesto Values for a BA

Everyone has read the four Agile Manifesto values at least once. But asked to look at a
real workplace scenario and say "which value is this", most people stall — because every
scenario seems to fit all four.

This topic is not about memorising the four values. It is about **discriminating** between
them, which is exactly what an agile business analysis assessment tests: read the scenario,
find the verb, pick the value.

## What the agile mindset is — and what it is not

The agile mindset is not a set of ceremonies. It is three repeated behaviours:

- **Continuous collaboration** between technical, business and customer representatives —
  not collaboration once at kickoff and never again.
- **Getting user feedback as often as possible** — feedback is a source of information,
  not an acceptance formality.
- **Delivering small, high-quality increments frequently** — small so you learn fast, high
  quality so what you shipped does not come back to bite you.

And here is the behaviour that is **not** part of the agile mindset, however common it is:

> Expecting that all requirements work is **complete and approved** before handing off to
> the development team.

Detailing everything up front and then handing off is precisely the model the Agile
Extension to the BABOK Guide was written against. It is not wrong because documentation is
bad — it is wrong because it assumes you know everything at the moment you know the least.

## The four values of the Agile Manifesto

| Value | The side that weighs more | The side that still has value |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Individuals and interactions | over processes and tools |
| 2 | Working software | over comprehensive documentation |
| 3 | Customer collaboration | over contract negotiation |
| 4 | Responding to change | over following a plan |

For a BA, the four translate into daily work like this:

- **Individuals and interactions** — you go and talk to users instead of mailing out a
  requirements form; you put people at the centre of the solution.
- **Working software** — you arrange a 20-minute demo instead of writing a 30-page
  specification to get the same answer.
- **Customer collaboration** — you sit with the customer to understand the feedback rather
  than citing the signed scope.
- **Responding to change** — you reorder the backlog when usage data says the original
  assumption was wrong.

## The word "over" — why the right-hand items still matter

**"Over"** is the most skipped word in the Manifesto. It does not say documentation is
useless, or that planning is pointless. It says: when the two sides conflict and you must
choose, the left-hand item wins.

There is a very practical consequence when you sit an assessment:

> An option that claims **documentation, process, contracts or plans have no value** is
> wrong on that basis alone. The Manifesto never said that.

The consequence at work is the same: an agile BA still writes documentation. The question is
never "do we document" but "how much is enough for the team to understand and for a decision
to be made".

## The verb decides the answer

This is the single most important discriminator in this topic. When you read a scenario,
do not ask "what is this about" — the topic is almost always *customer feedback*, and that
fits all four values. Ask instead: **what is the person DOING with the feedback.**

| If the scenario is about… | The value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Gathering or discussing feedback with the customer | Customer collaboration over contract negotiation | The emphasis is the relationship and the joint conversation |
| Acting on feedback — adding, removing, reordering the backlog | Responding to change over following a plan | The emphasis is changing the plan because of what was learned |
| Showing a demo instead of writing a document; questioning whether a document is worth its cost | Working software over comprehensive documentation | The emphasis is that the working increment is the better feedback instrument |
| Putting people at the centre; working closely with stakeholders along the whole value chain | Individuals and interactions over processes and tools | The emphasis is human interaction rather than an artefact or a process |

Note something subtle: in a well-written question all four options are **defensible in
isolation**. The assessment does not ask "which option is correct" but "which is **most
strongly evident**". The recurring phrasing in the official explanations is: *"this value
may relate to the scenario, but there is a better answer."*

## The pair that trips people up

These two values both concern customer feedback, which is why they get swapped most often.
The boundary is where the story stops:

- The BA and Product Owner **sit with the customer to understand** the feedback and discuss
  what it means → **Customer collaboration**.
- The BA and Product Owner **take the feedback into the backlog**, adding items, removing
  items, reordering priorities → **Responding to change**.

Same story, two different stopping points, two different answers. If the scenario ends at
the *conversation*, it is collaboration. If it ends at the *changed backlog*, it is
responding to change.

A second pair also gets confused: **Working software vs Individuals and interactions**.
When a BA persuades a stakeholder that a demo will yield better feedback than a detailed
document, there genuinely is human interaction — but what is being weighed is *a document
against a working increment*, so the answer is **Working software**.

## Self-check: read the scenario, pick the value

Try these four before you attempt the questions. Cover the right-hand column.

| Scenario | Value |
|---|---|
| Amy works with decision makers each iteration to remove things, add things and update the order of importance of the backlog | Responding to change |
| A BA seeks ongoing feedback from users and stakeholders to learn how well the solution is satisfying their needs | Customer collaboration |
| Stella explains that the team can get a working demo in front of the stakeholder faster than a detailed document, and that feedback on the demo will be worth more | Working software |
| Working closely with stakeholders at every step of the value chain, putting people at the centre of creating solutions | Individuals and interactions |

If you missed one, go back to the verb table above — you almost certainly chose on the
*topic* of the scenario rather than the *action*.

## Key takeaways

- Agile mindset = continuous collaboration + frequent feedback + small, high-quality
  increments. Completing and approving all requirements before hand-off is **not** part of it.
- The word **"over"** keeps the right-hand items valuable. Any option calling documentation
  or planning worthless is wrong.
- Read the **verb**, not the topic: *discussing* feedback → Customer collaboration;
  *putting it in the backlog and reordering* → Responding to change; *a demo instead of a
  document* → Working software; *putting people at the centre* → Individuals and interactions.
- All four options are plausible; you pick the **most evident** one, not the only true one.
