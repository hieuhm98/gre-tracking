# The Three Agile Analysis Horizons: Strategy, Initiative, Delivery

If you have time to learn only one topic from the *Agile Extension to the BABOK Guide v2*,
learn this one. The three **analysis horizons** are the frame that organises all of an agile
BA's work, and they are also the most heavily assessed area — roughly one question in five.

The good news: the wrong answers here are largely mechanical. A large share of distractors
are simply **names that are not horizons at all**.

## Three horizons — and only three

There are **exactly three** analysis horizons:

1. **Strategy horizon.** Which initiatives should the enterprise invest in, and why?
2. **Initiative horizon.** How should this initiative be shaped and sequenced to reach the
   outcome?
3. **Delivery horizon.** What exactly do we build next, and did it work?

The three are not sequential phases. They run **concurrently**: while the team is delivering
this iteration, the initiative is still being resequenced and the portfolio is still being
evaluated. A BA can move between all three in the same week.

## The master comparison

|  | Strategy horizon | Initiative horizon | Delivery horizon |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Timeframe** | 6–12 months and beyond | About 3 months | 1–4 weeks |
| **Question it answers** | Which initiatives should the enterprise invest in, and why? | How should this initiative be shaped and sequenced to reach the outcome? | What exactly do we build next, and did it work? |
| **Level of detail** | Goals, metrics, portfolio | Features, epics, release ordering | User stories, acceptance criteria, tasks |
| **Typical BA focus** | Observing change across a broad spectrum, reducing complexity in decision-making, enterprise-wide impact | Whether solution components align to real outcomes; how robust each feature must be and in what order; exploring alternatives | Preparing stories for implementation, supporting delivery, participating in retrospectives |
| **Who the BA works with** | Executives and portfolio decision-makers | The Product Owner, other initiative teams | The delivery team, customers and stakeholders directly |
| **How feedback is used** | Uses feedback from **all three horizons** to feed strategy | Uses feedback to reorder features and re-evaluate progress toward the outcome | Uses feedback from reviews and real usage to refine the next stories |
| **Explicitly NOT the BA's job here** | Mapping which user stories belong to which features | Ensuring the defined scope is feasible for a defined budget and schedule | Deciding whether the initiative should be cancelled |

The three timeframes are worth memorising outright: **1–4 weeks / 3 months / 6–12 months**.
They appear verbatim in questions, and "daily" corresponds to no horizon at all.

## Names that are not horizons

The assessment repeatedly offers a fourth option that sounds official but does not exist.
Learn this list and you can eliminate an option instantly on a great many questions:

- ~~Project horizon~~ — does not exist
- ~~Iteration horizon~~ — an iteration is not a horizon
- ~~Portfolio horizon~~ — a portfolio is not a horizon
- ~~Epic horizon~~ — an epic is not a horizon
- ~~Release horizon~~ — not a horizon
- ~~User story horizon~~ — not a horizon

The stronger general rule: if an option uses a term that **does not appear in the Agile
Extension v2**, it is almost always wrong. This holds beyond horizons too — "business value
map" and "design spike" are inventions of the same kind.

## Which horizon does this activity belong to?

| Activity or cue in the question | Horizon |
|---|---|
| Story elaboration | Delivery |
| Planning the details of the coming iteration in a planning workshop | Delivery |
| Analysis done directly with customers and stakeholders over a short timeframe | Delivery |
| Deciding the order of features to implement next, using new usage information | Initiative |
| Checking that solution components are aligned to getting real outcomes | Initiative |
| Backlog refinement — prioritising and sequencing features, ongoing and repeated | Initiative |
| Thinking about how a small change may affect the whole enterprise | Strategy |
| Setting organisational goals and the metrics that measure them | Strategy |
| Deciding which initiatives to invest in | Strategy |

A fast reading heuristic: find the **unit of work** in the sentence. Talking about *stories
and tasks* → delivery. Talking about *features, epics, releases* → initiative. Talking about
*initiatives, goals, the portfolio* → strategy.

## Techniques that appear in more than one horizon

Two techniques are exploited deliberately because they live in several horizons at once.

**Planning workshops exist in all three horizons**, differing only in level of detail:

| Horizon | The planning workshop focuses on |
|---|---|
| Strategy | Organisational goals, the metrics that measure those goals, and which initiatives can deliver value toward them |
| Initiative | The order of features and the release plan; creating a shared understanding of the approach to constructing the solution |
| Delivery | The details of the coming iteration — the tasks needed to complete it |

**Value modeling** depends on what it is being used for:

- Used to look at customer value **within one specific initiative** → the **initiative and
  delivery** horizons.
- Used to inform research and development, marketing and sales, looking at the customers of
  the **organisation as a whole** → the **strategy** horizon.

**Impact mapping** differs from both: it ties day-to-day activity back to organisational
goals, so it enhances the feedback loops between **all three horizons**.

The general lesson: read what the workshop or model is being used **FOR**, not just its name.

## What the BA is explicitly NOT doing at each horizon

A distinct question type asks the inverse: *which activity is NOT part of this horizon?*
Three answers are worth remembering, from the last row of the comparison table, and each is
wrong because it belongs to a different horizon:

- **At the strategy horizon, the BA does not** map which user stories belong to which
  features — that is the initiative horizon.
- **At the initiative horizon, the BA does not** ensure the defined scope is feasible for a
  defined budget and schedule — that is the older project frame, not the initiative focus.
- **At the delivery horizon, the BA does not** decide whether the initiative should be
  cancelled — that is a strategy-horizon decision.

Conversely, at the delivery horizon the BA **does** get stories ready for implementation,
**does** support successful delivery, and **does** participate in retrospectives.

There is also a question from the leadership angle: at the strategic level, the person who
owns the product helps the delivery team by **defining the product roadmap, identifying the
desired business value, and identifying the principles used to prioritise the work** — not
by fixing the first phase's budget, the technical context, or the release plan.

## Key takeaways

- Three horizons: **strategy (6–12 months+) · initiative (~3 months) · delivery (1–4 weeks)**.
  "Daily" is not a horizon.
- Project / iteration / portfolio / epic / release / user story are **not horizons** —
  eliminate on sight.
- Read the **unit of work**: story/task → delivery; feature/epic/release → initiative;
  initiative/goal/portfolio → strategy.
- **Planning workshops exist in all three**; **value modeling** is strategy when it looks at
  the whole organisation and initiative + delivery when it looks inside one initiative;
  **impact mapping** links all three.
- The strategy horizon uses feedback from **all** horizons to feed strategy.
